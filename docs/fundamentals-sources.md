## Primary statutory and regulatory text checked

- [26 U.S.C. §1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1202): current text, including 2025 amendment/effective-date notes. Sections (a), (b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), and (j) checked for the relevant claims.
- [26 U.S.C. §1223](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1223): especially exercise of stock rights in (5) and rollover holding-period carryover in (13).
- [26 U.S.C. §83](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/83): (b) election deadline and (f) holding period.
- [26 CFR §1.83-4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.83-4): holding period begins just after substantial vesting or, with a valid 83(b) election, just after transfer.
- [26 CFR §1.1202-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.1202-2): redemption windows, de minimis tests, and exceptions.
- [26 U.S.C. §1045](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1045): more than six months, 60-day replacement period, election, basis reduction, and special holding-period conditions.
- [26 U.S.C. §1(h)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1#h): (4) and (7) explain the special 28%-rate category for gain not excluded solely because of the Section 1202 percentage limitation.
- [IRS 2025 Schedule D instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sd): corroborates core qualification requirements, historical acquisition-date percentages, and the $50M/$75M issuance-date split. Its holding-period discussion still describes the older more-than-five-year rule, so the new tiers are sourced directly to the amended statute instead.

## Important distinctions preserved in copy

1. The acquisition-date split is on/before July 4, 2025 versus after July 4, 2025. Older shares require **more than five years**; newer shares have **at least** three/four/five-year tiers. Earlier acquisition eras retain their historical percentages. The guide notes the limited empowerment-zone exception.
2. Acquisition date determines the exclusion regime and dollar cap; **issuance date** determines the expanded gross-assets threshold. Holding-period carryover rules can keep later-transferred shares in the older regime.
3. Gross assets are a statutory asset measure, not enterprise valuation. Contributed property, predecessors, controlled corporations, and proceeds received at issuance affect the test.
4. The cap limits **eligible gain** before applying the percentage. Prior eligible gain, not only the amount actually excluded, reduces the remaining dollar cap. Older and newer dollar caps coordinate and are not additive.
5. Ten times basis uses qualifying basis in shares of the same issuer sold in that tax year, disregarding additions after original issuance for that calculation.
6. Newer dollar limits and asset thresholds have inflation adjustments after 2026. Future amounts are not invented. The statute's rule for certain exhausted dollar limits is flagged.
7. Nonexcluded gain from a partial exclusion and gain above the dollar/basis cap are different categories. Examples calculate excluded dollars rather than pretending to produce a complete tax bill.
8. Qualification requires multiple issuer, shareholder, stock, and transaction tests. No example certifies eligibility, no checklist result claims to be a determination, and the calculator is linked as illustrative only.

All numeric examples use hypothetical direct acquisitions, assume qualifying shares and sufficient supporting facts, and explicitly identify excluded and nonexcluded gain. No precise anniversary sale date is prescribed.
